## Project statement

Paul Murphy's original *MEMENTO MORI* treatment begins with a small, ugly object: a dead lawyer's fake award. His family can recognize the fraud immediately. Recognizing what he did to them, and what he did for other people, is harder.

The treatment connects the mementos Eli Mori leaves his daughter with the life he has spent explaining away. Callie has learned to treat every gift as a trap and every joke as an exit. Investigating the estate offers her a familiar pleasure: she can turn a difficult father into a case she knows how to prosecute. Then the case acquires people who will not behave like evidence.

The dual timeline gives that resistance a dramatic form. Caleb Caldwell cannot exist only as a discovery in Eli's file. His household has work to do, plans to argue about, children to raise and decisions to make before the lawyers' mistake becomes the center of the story. In the present, Daniel and Sarah cannot be the grateful beneficiaries who complete Eli's apology. They can disagree with each other and refuse what the Mori family wants.

The developed series keeps the treatment's family humor, estate puzzle and objects while testing the comfort of a final explanation. A father may have been brave and still have been cruel. Financial help may have mattered without paying a debt. Truth can be necessary and still become an instrument of control when the wrong person decides how it must be told.

The intended ending leaves the living with responsibility rather than an ingenious posthumous solution. Callie has to make a choice her father cannot script, at a cost he cannot reimburse. Understanding him changes the choice; it does not make it easy.
