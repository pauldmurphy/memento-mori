# MEMENTO MORI

**A ten-episode family mystery and historical drama**  
**Original treatment by Paul Murphy · Canyon House Pictures**

Owner: Lauren West (`lauren`). Version: pitch-v1, 2026-10-30. Status: development copy for Alex's independent creative-consistency review and approval. Based on the approved provisional ten-episode [season architecture](../development/season/season-break.md); the pilot outline and screenplay have separate approval gates. The 1965/2025 setting and expanded character design remain creative proposals until Alex records the corresponding canon decision. This is fiction, not an account of a documented Caldwell case.

## Logline

When an evasive lawyer leaves his estranged daughter a collection of insulting objects and names two strangers in his will, she sets out to expose his secret family—then discovers the strangers are the children of a murdered client, and her father's final confession may be another attempt to control them.

## The show

Eli Mori dies in an ordinary car wreck with a fake humanitarian award in his glove compartment. His widow hides it. His daughter Callie catches her. The mourning has barely started and the family is already handling evidence.

Callie is a lawyer because her parents were very effective at making their wishes feel like her choices. Now Eli has left one last instruction manual: objects for his children, money routed elsewhere, two unknown beneficiaries named Daniel and Sarah Caldwell, and a sealed explanation Leon, his former partner, will not simply hand over. Callie reaches the most satisfying conclusion available. Her father had another family. She intends to prove it.

The series moves between an Alabama household in 2025, where grief becomes a fight over money and reputation, and Birmingham in 1965, where Caleb Caldwell and his wife Marian are trying to build a life with newborn twins. Caleb wants to challenge what has been done to him. Marian wants a say in the risks his lawyers consider acceptable. Eli sees a case that could make him matter. Leon knows how differently the same room can treat two lawyers.

The past is an active story of work, marriage, ambition and danger. The present is a struggle over what anyone may do with its surviving record. A will contest can pressure the Mori family; it cannot make the Caldwells accept an interview, surrender a letter or bless a public account. Daniel wants his family beyond Eli's reach. Sarah refuses to pretend the help they received meant nothing. Neither owes Callie the explanation she came to collect.

Across ten episodes, private meetings, interrupted access, a family dinner, a disputed account and an uncut photograph change the balance of power. Callie loses professional advantage by respecting a limit she could once have argued around. Ruth has to decide whether protecting Eli has become her whole life. Leon's account puts his own choices into the record without making his responsibility identical to Eli's.

The season ends with living people rejecting a dead man's ready-made settlement. Money and publication remain separate. Daniel keeps a private account; Sarah permits a narrower one. Callie gives up part of her inheritance and professional standing without purchasing forgiveness. The mystery finds its answer in what the family chooses to keep—and what it finally stops claiming to own.

## Viewing promise

Family wit sharp enough to draw blood, a mystery that changes who holds the power, and a historical story whose people have lives beyond becoming someone else's secret. Paul's tonal references, *Succession* and *Mississippi Burning*, are creative coordinates: intimate family combat colliding with danger that a clever argument cannot contain. Ten approximately hour-long episodes with a complete ending is the working format; no continuation is required to resolve this season.
