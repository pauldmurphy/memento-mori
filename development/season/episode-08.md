## Synopsis

The office box Eli hid for Callie offers exactly the emotional answer she has wanted to disbelieve. Unsent cards, drafted apologies and the list of arguments he could not stop having reveal a father who cared and kept controlling the exchange. The intact photograph shows Caleb alive between the young lawyers, with an expression no confession can reduce. Eli's plain note names the trust he failed to bear.

Callie begins constructing the definitive explanation before realizing she is doing it again. The circulating cut photograph is a separate physical print. The intact one does not prove who cut the other or grant the family a public image right. She offers a private review and a specific copy instead of an exhibit.

Daniel first wants the image left without representation, beyond Eli's notes and Callie's meaning. Then he sees that a blank caption leaves the lawyer's sentence dominant. He writes a first-person note for the private Caldwell copy. He cannot remember a father who died when he was ten weeks old. He distinguishes Marian's stories, his own later imagining and actual childhood/adult encounters with Eli. A practical kindness and an enduring insult both stay. He commits to leave the note with the image even if it disrupts Sarah's view.

Sarah separately keeps her family copy without authorizing publication. Their disagreement remains a living sibling relationship, no longer a binary button Callie must obtain. Daniel has become an author at personal cost, not a reluctant approving witness.

Marian's historical choice is equally specific. She rejects a memorial caption that would make Caleb the client who completed the lawyers' courage. She keeps an ordinary identification with a family print and leaves room for the children's questions. She cannot dictate every later answer, but she can prevent the copy she controls from carrying Eli's preferred interpretation.

Leon discloses one remaining paper. After the hidden box has made Eli more human, its offer of a clean future will be harder to refuse. Callie has found a wound, not a mandate.

6. Daniel initially declines representation, then chooses to write his own private account.
7. He explicitly separates inherited Marian stories from actual later memories of Eli.
8. He keeps a complicating kindness and injury in the note rather than curate purity.
9. Sarah receives a separate private copy; neither act authorizes naming or publication.
10. Historical Marian rejects Eli's caption and retains a plainly identified family print.
11. Callie can keep only a specific private copy under the eventual agreement.
12. End: Leon's last paper arrives after emotional explanation has made its false simplicity attractive.
