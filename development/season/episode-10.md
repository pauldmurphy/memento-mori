## Synopsis

Independent counsel brings the living estate parties to separate money terms. Ruth remains in her home. Ben and Leah accept deferred liquidity instead of forcing sale. Sarah accepts a negotiated allocation that includes half of Callie's own discretionary share. Daniel declines new personal money. The single contest is withdrawn through counsel as the financial dispute is resolved. Routine administration may continue, but the money conflict no longer holds the story or the records hostage.

The record arrangement has different terms. Daniel receives a private Caldwell archive, keeps his own note and refuses Eli's letter, public portrait, public attribution and memorial participation. Sarah accepts her letter and selected copies. Having tested the redactions, she rejects the proposed public family exhibit. She authorizes a narrower personal account of her own encounters with an unnamed benefactor, using selected correspondence she controls, with no names, dates, locality, portrait, twin relation or distinctive ledger detail identifying Daniel. She acknowledges residual recognition risk rather than receive an impossible guarantee. No permission speaks for the image-maker or the other dead and living people.

Leon hands over only the agreed packet and his qualified account. His annotation states limits; it does not grant Callie a final verdict. Callie signs the money terms with Eli's pen and feels a private name echo without treating it as proof that her life was engineered. Her career setback remains. She leaves the firm's preferred route on unfavorable terms and has no prestigious replacement waiting to reward the decision.

Ruth offers the award, including the concealed dedication to Caleb. The hidden words do not cancel the false public face. Callie's small memorial is an honest account of her father rather than disclosure of the private Caldwell material. Daniel is absent, without a secret approving reaction shot. Sarah's decisions do not require her to bless the Moris' grief.

In the 1966 coda Marian opens the reading room with other women holding keys and a duplicate ledger. Caleb's imperfect repair remains a practical thing they work around. The room is not compensation for his life. It is work that continues.

Callie places the award beside the specifically permitted private photograph copy. The original and Daniel's writing are elsewhere. She does not fill the empty shelf space. The series has an estate ending, a family ending and enacted public/private decisions. Historical uncertainty remains honest uncertainty, not a cliffhanger.
