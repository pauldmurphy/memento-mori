## Proposed, awaiting creative review

Approximately hour-long episodes; limited-series positioning with a complete ending.

Alex recommends a ten-episode design with three interacting engines: a time-bound estate contest, a present fight over control of the Caleb record, and an active historical story driven by Caleb, his wife, Eli and Leon. Callie remains the lead, with Daniel or Sarah elevated to a co-driving present-day protagonist. The Caldwell family should control decisions about its own history rather than function only as evidence in the Mori family's reckoning.

Responsibility for the historical failure should be shared but explicitly unequal: Eli and Leon both make choices, while their racial exposure, access, coercion and consequences differ. The exact causal and legal mechanism must remain open until sourced historical and procedural research is complete.

Alex recommends removing the late alternative will as the controlling solution. An earlier-seeded draft or defective contingency document may reveal Eli's hope, but the living parties should create the binding settlement and Daniel and Sarah must be able to refuse. Callie's ending should echo rather than duplicate the original silence: preserve an honest record, give the Caldwell family informed veto power and impose a concrete cost on Callie.

Provisional research option: test 1965/2025 because the source specifies a 1965 pen and a sixty-year echo. These dates, all ages, the historical and present jurisdictions, the widow's identity, the underlying case, probate/disclosure mechanics, and the precise season architecture remain unapproved and unverified.
