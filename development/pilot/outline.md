# MEMENTO MORI — Pilot scene outline

**Episode 1: The Ordinary Wreck**  
Charlie Reed (`charlie`) · outline-v1 · 2026-10-30  
Original treatment by Paul Murphy. **Proposed fiction, submitted for review; not yet approved for screenplay.**

Read with the [beat sheet](beat-sheet.md) for the proposal/source distinction, invented work dispute, MM-DP-1 age/name proposal, timing targets and withholding budget. Scene numbers are development references. These are prose scenes, not screenplay or dialogue samples. No legal mechanism is represented as verified history or an effective result. Any approved full dialogue draft will be UTF-8 Fountain. Historical scenes are authored fiction; their existence does not imply Callie has access to them or Leon accurately remembers them.

## Sequence 1 — A manageable death (0–9 minutes)

### 01. Roadside and impound intake — present, late afternoon

An already completed ordinary collision: damaged cars, a driver giving a statement, workers wanting the road open. No pursuer, tampered component or suspicious witness. Eli's mess is a takeaway cup, dropped glasses and pharmacy bag. Ruth arrives to identify his things, conducting this like an errand with a signature at the end. A staff member gives her a property bag; an ugly humanitarian plaque falls against the plastic. She turns it inward before its title becomes a story somebody tells. Tending the version of Eli has begun before she has absorbed his death. Cut from her signature to Callie initialing work on a screen.

### 02. Callie's borrowed conference room — present, same afternoon

Callie closes an argument with a junior colleague by revising one precise word in a client promise. She is good at work she resents. Ruth's call turns competence useless: no negotiation improves what Ruth says. Callie asks practical questions until Ruth needs her daughter rather than an administrator. Callie promises to come, then finishes the marked sentence before leaving. The colleague offers to cover; Callie sends the file anyway. A career habit defeats an offered exit. Her phone keeps vibrating after she shuts the computer.

### 03. Caldwell kitchen — Birmingham, proposed 1965, morning

Caleb tries to eat, repair a chair rung and leave for the depot while infant twins unsettle the household's timing. Marian needs him to carry a deposit for her paid accounts; he refuses to mix client money with his work errand. She gives him only their short-pay envelope. Their teasing concerns sleep and which repairs last. His next shifts have disappeared and he intends to get an explanation. Marian wants the loss put in numbers before anger spends another day. He takes the pay record, leaving her clients' ledger behind. A ledger, not a lawyer, controls the first decision.

### 04. Mori kitchen — present, after burial, early evening

The house reflects this family's chosen mourning practice: low chairs, neighbors' food, an old prayer book Ruth selected, a mirror awkwardly covered with the wrong-sized cloth. These are household choices, not universal ritual claims. Ruth hides the plaque in a crowded drawer and must remove a useful utensil. A neighbor asks which mug was Eli's. Ruth cannot choose between three. The front door opens; she composes herself before turning. She wants to get through the evening without becoming an exhibit.

### 05. Mori front step and hall — present, continuous

Callie arrives with a bag too small for an indefinite stay. Ben and Leah have been collecting food, calling relatives and fixing the bathroom latch. Her brisk offer to reorganize lands as criticism. They give her an actual job: sit with Ruth. Callie sees memorial cards awaiting wording and chooses those instead. Leah removes them from her hands. The family can still make her laugh, irritating her more than hostility. Nobody can give her a task that substitutes for arriving.

### 06. Kitchen — present, minutes later

Callie seeks a serving utensil and finds the award. Ruth wants the drawer closed; Callie wants an explanation for the misspelling, wrong date and grandiose title. Her joke begins as an intimacy bid and becomes a weapon when Ruth does not laugh. Ruth asks that the dead be allowed a kindness. Callie tests whether the issuing organization is familiar; Ruth's overquick retrieval makes the plaque matter. Callie gives it back rather than win a tug of war. She has caught concealment, not proved fraud. Ruth retains it. A mourner's knock stops the question without ending it.

### 07. Living room — present, early evening

Ruth wants a short memory from each child for a later memorial. Ben supplies an ordinary failure that became a family joke; Leah wants an actual tenderness preserved. Callie cannot keep her memory from turning into a deposition about a broken promise. Ruth gives her permission to say nothing. Callie accepts too quickly and offers to handle the will instead. Ruth says that is Leon's business for another time. Callie's substitute for grief has become a forbidden room, therefore a goal. Carry the silence into a depot where Caleb waits to be admitted.

## Sequence 2 — The man people brought (9–19 minutes)

### 08. Fictional freight depot, service counter — historical, morning

Caleb wants missing pay and a date to return to the repair floor. A clerk refers him to a supervisor already gone; a replacement passes behind the counter carrying tools Caleb used. He controls his temper long enough to obtain a carbon of the work/pay entry. He alleges the trouble began when the supervisor saw Marian; the scene supplies no omniscient confirmation of motive. A coworker can corroborate shifts but declines to put his name on paper. Caleb leaves with a document, without wages, work or the witness he expected. Choosing a record costs a needed relationship.

### 09. Mori living room — present, evening

A former client wants Ruth to know Eli once stayed all night until a family member was released. Callie wants the date and missing part of the story. Another guest remembers a fee dispute. The woman offering comfort refuses to let Callie cross-examine her memory into worthlessness and moves to Ruth's chair. Callie gains no fact and loses the right to be the most injured person in the room. Ben hands her empty plates, giving her a route away. Laughter elsewhere makes Eli seem alive for an unwelcome second.

### 10. Back porch — present, continuous

Callie answers a firm call about a prospective referral she has cultivated. The caller offers time; she insists she can deliver a short intake assessment tomorrow. Ben overhears and mistakes work for strength, asking her to handle family paperwork too. She snaps that competence is how they volunteer her life. He points to his wet sleeves and food deliveries: she is not the only one working through this. He goes inside without rescuing her from the call. The later referral cost is seeded as an actual opportunity, not yet lost.

### 11. Small law-office workroom — historical, late morning

Caleb waits while Leon helps another client with a practical problem: a functioning community room, not two lawyers waiting for his grand case. Caleb wants a demand that restores his job. Eli sees a larger injustice and starts building an account with publicity in mind. Caleb enjoys being taken seriously until Eli moves from wages to a representative story. Leon asks who must enter the depot after the lawyers leave. Caleb sets his first condition: he wants a job he can return to, not an escape chosen for him. They need Marian's records before promising a move.

### 12. Mori hall — present, evening

Leon arrives tired, formally dressed, with a case he declines to place in Callie's offered hands. Ruth's greeting is intimate and practiced; Callie sees shorthand she never shared. He wants to pay respects before business. Callie asks about the will. He has copies to explain, not an answer that improves tonight. Leah invites him to sit; he does. Callie's pressure makes her discourteous at her own father's mourning. The guarded case stays in sight without becoming a thriller bomb.

### 13. Caldwell work table — historical, midday

Marian must finish a paid reconciliation before the household eats into its reserve. A bookkeeping client brings receipts late and wants the reading-room pledges turned into books. Marian will not make restricted contributions a household loan or count promises as cash. She risks seeming ungenerous by refusing an inflated total. Caleb returns and volunteers her records for the lawyers without asking. She closes the client ledger and agrees to bring their own account instead. The woman sees the disagreement; Marian now has a community witness who expects her to hold the boundary.

### 14. Hall outside Eli's office — present, evening

Callie catches Leon retrieving a sealed packet left during a prior ordinary visit. She asks why he may enter a room Ruth kept her out of. He identifies personal instructions and refuses to open them in the hall. He claims no court order, only his own undertaking. Callie demands that they at least hear the will tonight. Ruth agrees to a short private explanation after visitors leave that room. Callie gets the meeting, and responsibility for its timing.

## Sequence 3 — The beneficiaries (19–29 minutes)

### 15. Dining room — present, later evening

Food is cleared from one end of a table still surrounded by mourning. Leon sets a limited purpose: explain a copy, identify questions for estate counsel, distribute nothing. Ruth wants privacy. Callie closes the doors and takes notes; Ben and Leah want household bills paid. Leon cannot collapse several financial structures into one pot. Callie initially treats that limit as Eli's theatricality. No court findings, admission date or contest period are read. It is an arranged family meeting, not a probate ceremony.

### 16. Dining room — present, continued

Leon lays out specified objects: watch, fountain pen, worn file cover, broken chess clock, folded photograph and menorah. A bequest makes Ben laugh, then resent Eli anticipating the laugh. Leah remembers helping repair the clock and refuses to litigate the memory. Callie receives the pen and file cover as identified items, not keys to all records. The photograph remains folded among common material; no missing person's identity appears. Their grievances are specific enough that Eli cannot be the same father for all three.

### 17. Church workroom — historical, afternoon

Marian and two women measure shelving and sort donated books, finding few usable for the ages they hoped to serve. She wants a reading room that can pay for light and repairs; a collaborator wants it opened with what they have. Caleb, collecting her, offers to build shelves but cannot promise lumber without wages. Marian chooses a smaller opening rather than misuse earmarked money. Delaying his fight delays something of hers too. Her project has money, conflict and a decision Eli never hears.

### 18. Dining room — present, continuous with 16

Leon explains the storage reference, named trusts and charity-like entity no one recognizes. Eli's requested dinner is a wish with personal meaning; he makes no claim that refusal forfeits inheritance. Jokes about whether the dead can book a restaurant turn to argument about expected guests. Callie wants a financial schedule Leon has not offered. He shares a copy while distinguishing what he holds from material elsewhere. Her inventory makes her look in control just before the names arrive.

### 19. Dining room — present, moments later

Leon reads Daniel and Sarah Caldwell as named beneficiaries. Ruth looks down before the second name. Callie notices, asks whether they are relatives and receives no simple answer. Leon knows them as people but will not give their histories across the table. Callie treats refusal as concession. Ben and Leah stop joking. Unknown people have converted a ridiculous estate into a threat to who belonged to this family.

### 20. Dining room — present, continuous — MIDPOINT

Callie makes the hidden-family theory unmistakable, wanting Ruth to deny it. Ruth wants her children to stop making strangers carry their father's failures. She acknowledges knowing the Caldwells but will not turn that into a paternity story. Leon halts the meeting when Callie reaches for his packet. Ben/Leah have heard an accusation, Ruth has been wounded and Callie mistakes disruption for progress. A question circulates like a family fact though nobody has answered it.

### 21. Kitchen — present, aftermath

Ruth tidies as if order can repair the room. Callie offers to bear the scandal for her and expects gratitude for a rescue Ruth did not request. Ruth asks whether she came to sit with her or extract something. Callie says those cannot be separate when Eli made everything a condition. Ruth closes the award drawer in front of her. Callie sees an exhibit; Ruth sees her daughter choosing Eli's prosecutorial posture. Neither names the deeper fear. An infant crying takes us to Marian before she can leave for a meeting.

## Sequence 4 — What a name can take (29–40 minutes)

### 22. Caldwell kitchen — historical, late afternoon

Caleb wants Marian ready before his nerve or the office hours run out. She must settle paid work, arrange twins' care and deliver the reading-room total. He treats those as delay until she asks which task he can take. He holds one baby, then both, while she finishes. Their practical exchange becomes the real argument: he wants open recognition; she wants work and income that are not collateral. Sharing anger does not authorize every remedy. They take their own pay record and leave her clients' ledger closed.

### 23. Mori dining room — present, evening

Callie builds a small index and asks for the copies her siblings received. Ben agrees; Leah wants an answer before helping and keeps her object. Callie can photograph only offered pages. She labels a column with the hidden-family theory. Leah makes her cross it out: a name is not a relationship. Callie changes it to a question and continues sorting details toward the same answer. The investigation begins with voluntary fragments and a visible bias.

### 24. Eli's office — present, evening

Callie wants the family file to identify what Ruth knew. Ruth permits one folder and remains at the door. It contains Callie's youthful alternative-career application with Eli's legal-career annotations attached. Callie strips the paperclip off, then replaces it. Imposed direction is visible in handwriting and reflex, not a name-echo thesis. She almost tries a locked lower cabinet; Ruth says she has not offered that. The hidden box stays hidden. Callie leaves with the permitted folder and a grievance too large for it.

### 25. Small law-office workroom — historical, late afternoon

Eli has prepared a broad account before meeting Marian, hoping publicity makes the supervisor/company uncomfortable. Caleb wants tangible pressure. Marian sees her clients and church contacts listed as corroborating people. She asks why household addresses and her separate work belong in a pay dispute. Eli sees persuasive context. Leon asks whose consent they have. Availability has been mistaken for permission; Marian has a specific thing to stop.

### 26. Mori backyard — present, night

Leah wants help selecting one photograph of Eli with all three children for a later memorial. Callie keeps checking the bequest page and calls the photo task propaganda. Leah refuses the choice between a memory and an honest account. A recollection of Eli teaching a child to cheat badly at chess makes both laugh, then cry differently. Callie takes the photograph but returns to her index. Leah finishes alone. This is an emotional cost before future money or professional penalties.

### 27. Dining room and kitchen threshold — present, night

Callie asks Leon for contact with the Caldwells. He offers to pass on a request, not surrender a private directory. Ruth volunteers a number she has used to prevent a surprise visit, asking for a respectful approach. Callie hears confirmation of a longstanding second household. Leon warns that a person is not a discovery response. Callie assures them she knows how to ask a question. Her confidence makes the next scene a choice.

### 28. Mori front walk — present, night

Callie's voicemail to Sarah starts politely and then uses the idea of another family. Trying to sound able to handle an ugly truth, she sounds as though Sarah's role is already assigned. Ruth opens the door too late to stop the message sending. A private theory has become harm somebody else must absorb. There is no immediate answer. The dark phone leaves Callie listening to an ordinary house.

## Sequence 5 — The narrower move (40–50 minutes)

### 29. Small law-office workroom — historical, early evening

Marian removes the client sheet from the proposed packet. She permits Caleb's pay record and their account, refusing names, circulating their home address and exposing her separate work. The tactic loses its easy backdrop; a quicker remedy may be harder. Caleb accuses her of planning an exit while he tries to reclaim their place. She answers with work she means to keep, including tomorrow's paid reconciliation. She does not ask rescue from a brave man's choice. Eli must decide whether he can help without owning the story.

### 30. Workroom corridor — historical, continuous

Leon takes Caleb aside so the disagreement can be heard without another lawyer speaking over Marian. Caleb asks whether caution always means accepting a smaller life. Leon does not pretend equal exposure: he can remain in a professional room Caleb must leave to get paid. He admits wanting a case that matters, making caution less costless. Caleb chooses to return rather than walk out or authorize Eli behind Marian's back. Leon changes an action rather than lecturing about history.

### 31. Mori kitchen — present, late night

Ruth is alone with the plaque until Callie returns for water. Callie apologizes for tone without withdrawing the accusation. Ruth acknowledges knowing Daniel/Sarah before tonight and refuses to defend her marriage by disclosing their lives. Asked for one thing about Eli that was not managed, she gives an ordinary kindness with no evidentiary value. Callie wants belief but keeps examining it. Ruth moves the plaque to her own bag, ending the drawer search. Custody changes; the clue is not erased.

### 32. Dining room — present, late night

Leon packs while Callie demands instructions and storage key. He refuses demands dressed as entitlement. Callie threatens a challenge as if it unlocks the packet; Leon separates the matters. His personal undertaking concerns Eli's condition; court procedure needs independent advice. Pressed, he says Eli anticipated a formal challenge and left instructions about what Leon should then consider releasing. He will not release it now to relieve her anger. No video image or quoted message appears. Callie has learned the shape of a trap, not its contents.

### 33. Small law-office workroom — historical, evening

Eli revises the broad proposal in front of the family, cutting the list and specifying a narrower private follow-up. Eli resents lost momentum, Caleb lost public force, Marian having to object twice. Leon keeps only agreed material. Caleb will return with dates/amounts; Marian will discuss their household, not clients. No affidavit is sworn, court seals anything or filing occurs. The unsigned broad draft remains a trace of a proposal, not evidence of later implementation. Their next meeting must begin from a changed option.

### 34. Mori front hall — present, late night

Callie appeals to the adult who once translated Eli for her. Leon admits making Eli easier to live with and being unproud of everything that enabled. He does not confess Caldwell history. She hears weakness and presses; he ends the conversation. His affection is no longer an access route. Ruth offers leftovers and he takes them, a mundane mercy argument cannot consume. His case leaves with him. Nobody obtains it offscreen.

### 35. Caldwell street and kitchen — historical, night

They bring their papers home irritated by money a delay may cost. Caleb fixes a neighbor's broken latch while Marian sorts tomorrow's childcare; community life exists beyond the case. At home he makes a small shelf from salvaged wood. She does not let a gesture replace the argument. She marks paid work in her ledger; he rewrites shift dates with uncertain entries visibly marked. They choose separate work for a future together. No pursuer, threat or death image turns ordinary life into a countdown.

## Sequence 6 — Keep it open (50–58 minutes)

### 36. Mori dining room — present, pre-dawn

Callie's elegant index proves almost nothing. Her firm file waits beside it. She begins a polished estate assessment, then deletes a sentence treating the Caldwell relationship as established. A blank page separates questions: what the will says; missing facts; whether any lawful challenge is available; who might voluntarily share what. This is a professional correction, not moral enlightenment. Leah's family photograph sits outside the categories. Callie leaves it out of the evidence grid.

### 37. Childhood room — present, dawn

Packing to leave, Callie finds no room for Ruth's food and the file without removing something. She takes out the clothes meant for a short stay. She requests an appointment with independent estate counsel to assess whether a challenge could be available and what facts/admission status counsel must examine. She supplies no invented ground and requests no filing. This is a future appointment, not advice already obtained. She tells the firm the intake assessment will arrive later today: a harder day chosen, not a professional loss invented.

### 38. Kitchen — present, morning

Ruth sees Callie still there and assumes the investigation won. Callie offers breakfast and asks, unusually, how Ruth wants it. Ruth refuses to make that forgiveness. Callie requests voluntary storage access; Ruth cannot grant everything and will consider a limited visit with Leon. No key transfers or Caldwells' consent is promised; no court effect is implied. Ruth accepts breakfast. Callie must tolerate delay without converting it to an accusation.

### 39. Front step — present, morning

Sarah's response arrives. Stay with Callie; the adult Caldwells are not yet a second present plot. Sarah rejects the premise: Ruth's number was not permission to define Sarah's family. She will consider a private meeting if Callie first asks what Sarah wants from it. Daniel has agreed to nothing; Sarah promises neither his presence nor a collective position. Callie drafts a defense, deletes it and requests a conversation on those terms. Her original message cannot be recalled. Episode 2 inherits this damaged route.

### 40. Dining room / Caldwell kitchen — present morning / historical night

Callie replaces the hidden-family heading with two names and no relationship underneath. She sets down the pen unopened. Caleb repairs the imperfect shelf; Marian puts away her ledger; the twins finally sleep. He has kept space for books they do not own yet. Return to Callie finding the memorial photograph beside work. She lets an unresolved thing remain in the room. Ruth calls her to breakfast. She goes, leaving questions open. The hook is active: stay, assess a viable challenge, meet people who can refuse her account. Eli left a case; the living have not consented to be it.

## Controls for the screenplay gate

- No Episode 2 filing/admission event, video, Caldwell arrival, storage search or substantive paternity resolution. Sarah refuses the premise without narrating true history.
- Award's inscription, intact photograph, Caleb's fate, payments, secret box and contingency paper stay withheld. The depot complaint is invented, not documentary proof.
- Family meeting, personal instructions, voluntary access request, counsel appointment and historical follow-up are separate actions. Neither timeline acquires procedure by montage.
- Leon can stay in professional rooms; Caleb must seek wages; Marian's livelihood/client information is exposed; Eli can leave with the strategy. Participation is not equal exposure or responsibility.
- Mourning, siblings' work, food and failed intimacy have objectives and costs. Humor comes from relationships and avoidance, not ethnicity or historical danger.
