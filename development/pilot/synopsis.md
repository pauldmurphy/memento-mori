# MEMENTO MORI — Pilot synopsis

**The Ordinary Wreck** · pilot-v1 · 2026-10-30  
Original treatment and series creation by Paul Murphy. Accountable development writer: Charlie Reed (`charlie`), Canyon House Pictures AI team. This synopsis describes the actual [Fountain draft](memento-mori-pilot.fountain), submitted for Lauren's independent screenplay review and Alex's approval. It is not a production/runtime claim or historical account.

An ordinary road accident kills Eli Mori, an eighty-nine-year-old lawyer whose family has spent years trying to get a straight answer from him. Ruth, his widow, collects his effects and quietly conceals a cheap humanitarian award found in the car. His eldest daughter Callie receives the news at work and finishes one sentence before she can leave. The habit says as much as her grief will allow.

Days later, after the burial, Callie comes home for shiva. Her siblings Ben and Leah have been managing food, guests and broken fixtures. She wants a task that will excuse her from sitting still. She finds the award in Ruth's kitchen drawer and turns an attempted joke into an accusation. Other mourners remember an Eli she cannot reconcile with the evasive father she knew: generous, tireless, costly, impossible. Callie cross-examines their comfort until they stop offering it to her. A work call reminds her of the valuable referral she has pursued for months. She insists she can keep working through the loss.

In Birmingham in 1965, Caleb Caldwell, a Black mechanic with infant twins, needs missing wages and his shifts restored at a fictional freight-depot garage. He believes his supervisor changed his treatment after meeting his white wife, Marian. He gets a work record but no money and no coworker willing to stand beside him. At home, Marian keeps paid accounts and is helping other women build a lending-and-reading room. Their precarious household needs Caleb's remedy, but her own work and her clients' money are not his to offer.

Caleb takes his complaint to young Eli and Leon. Eli sees the possibility of public pressure and a case larger than the unpaid hours. Leon asks what Caleb wants to return to after the lawyers leave. Caleb wants work, dignity and an openly acknowledged family; he does not want to be made into a story about why he had to run. When Eli asks about household income, Caleb supplies names from Marian's work. Eli starts writing before anyone has asked her permission.

In the present, Leon arrives at the Mori house with a cane, assistance for the step and a case he retains control of. Callie persuades Ruth to let him explain the will privately that evening. The meeting begins with absurd objects and distinct memories: Ben's loose watch, Leah's broken chess clock, Callie's pen and empty file cover. Separate financial structures, storage references and Eli's requested dinner leave Callie reaching for a system she can decipher. Then Leon names two beneficiaries: Daniel and Sarah Caldwell. Ruth recognizes them before Callie can finish her question.

Callie announces her theory of another family. Ruth acknowledges knowing the Caldwells but refuses to let Callie invent their history. Leon ends the meeting when she reaches for his sealed instructions. Her siblings have heard an accusation nobody has confirmed, and Ruth has been forced to defend a marriage in the middle of mourning. Callie responds by organizing voluntary copies into an investigation. Leah makes her put a question mark beside the theory and later asks for help choosing a family photograph. Callie returns to her papers instead. An old career application, clipped to Eli's annotations steering her toward law, shows how thoroughly she learned this substitute for intimacy.

Marian's visit to the historical office changes Eli's intended move. She removes the client names and refuses circulation of their address or her separate ledger. Caleb resents losing public force; Marian resents being offered as evidence. Leon admits his own wish for a consequential case while recognizing that the couple must carry risks the lawyers can leave at the office. Caleb returns to the table rather than authorize Eli behind Marian's back. They agree to a narrower private follow-up. No filing occurs. At home, Caleb repairs a little shelf while Marian does paid work. They have not resolved the argument, but they have kept a future to work toward.

Ruth gives Callie Sarah's number in hopes of preventing a surprise visit. Callie's voicemail turns the hidden-family suspicion into harm a stranger must hear. Leon refuses to surrender his case or the storage key through intimidation, separating his personal undertaking from any possible legal challenge. Ruth offers one unremarkable memory of Eli: he used to warm her shoes. Callie cannot make that kindness an answer to her own injury.

Before dawn, Callie notices an earlier beneficiary copy and inconsistent scan details, then writes questions instead of a conclusion. She requests an appointment with independent counsel to assess what the papers establish and whether a challenge could be available; she files nothing. She stays, makes Ruth breakfast and asks for voluntary access that Ruth can only consider. Sarah's reply refuses Callie's premise and offers the possibility of a private meeting on Sarah's terms, without promising Daniel's participation. Callie sends a question in return. She remains hungry for an explanation, but the living have begun refusing the roles she assigned them.
