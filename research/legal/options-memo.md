## 1. Historical line: what 1965 Alabama can support, and what it cannot yet support

### Interracial marriage as pressure, not automatic case fact

**Documented law (federal, 1883 and 1967):** *Pace v. Alabama*, 106 U.S. 583 (1883), upheld Alabama's interracial-sex statute as then framed. The Supreme Court later held state bans on interracial marriage unconstitutional in *Loving v. Virginia*, 388 U.S. 1 (1967), including the legal regime that had left Alabama's ban on the books. Sources: [Pace, 106 U.S. 583](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/106/583/) (opinion, 1883); [Loving, 388 U.S. 1](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/388/1/) (opinion, June 12, 1967). **Status:** documented federal decisions; the linked sites reproduce opinions, not Alabama's archival 1965 code.

**Story option:** Caleb's marriage to a white woman can create real social and legal exposure in 1965 Birmingham. It does **not** by itself prove that police, prosecutors, or a court used a particular charge, nor that it caused his death. A case can instead involve intimidation, employment, housing, custody, police violence, or a civil-rights filing, with the marriage as one vulnerability among several.

**Open:** Obtain the 1965 Alabama Code, county docket/newspaper/archive records, and a period historian or Alabama lawyer's review before naming a charge, filing, courthouse practice, or causal sequence.

### Civil-rights litigation and the “sealed affidavit” device

**Open / unverified:** No source reviewed verifies a Birmingham case matching Caleb Caldwell, a sealed affidavit that leaked, or a threat-driven bargain by Eli and Leon. The treatment's phrase “they held the leverage to demand an explosive federal inquiry” is source-backed fiction, not a documented legal conclusion.

**Plausibility options:**

1. **Civil filing + discovery leakage:** the lawyers file a real civil-rights action and use a sworn statement or anticipated testimony as leverage. The danger is not that “sealed” automatically means secure; the story must specify who possessed it, what rule/order protected it, and how it escaped.
2. **Informal evidence custody:** the lawyers hold a witness statement, photograph, or police-related record outside a filed case. This is dramatically cleaner for custody and moral choice, but it is not automatically “suppression” or obstruction.
3. **Federal referral pressure:** they threaten referral or publicity rather than possess a guaranteed federal remedy. This avoids claiming that a private lawyer could command an investigation.

For all three, identify the actual forum, document, custodian, order, recipient, and date. Do not have a document's existence alone prove that it caused violence.

### Historical confidentiality and survivor protection

**Documented context, limited confidence:** Alabama's modern Rule 1.6 is not a 1965 rule. The Alabama Judicial System publishes the current [Rules of Professional Conduct](https://judicial.alabama.gov/library/RulesBarConduct), including Rule 1.6. A 1965 story may use lawyer-client confidence as professional context, but the exact governing Alabama bar canon, privilege doctrine, and exceptions must be checked in the 1965 code/reports and period ethics materials.

**Story option:** Eli and Leon can make a morally consequential decision not to disclose evidence because Caleb's widow and twins face an immediate threat. That is a fictional ethical dilemma. It should not be described as a legally authorized “settlement” of murder or as proof that silence legally protected them.

**Open:** Research period criminal exposure for withholding evidence, obstruction, misprision, conspiracy, intimidation, and civil liability; limitation/revival rules; federal/state jurisdiction; and whether a lawyer-client privilege, work product, or court order covered the material. The treatment does not establish any of these.

### Wrongful-death timing: a caution against retroactive easy remedies

**Documented statutory history (Alabama):** Current Ala. Code § 6-5-410(d) states a wrongful-death action must be commenced within two years, and the statute's history traces the rule through the 1940 Code, Title 7, § 123. Source: [Ala. Code § 6-5-410](https://codes.findlaw.com/al/title-6-civil-practice/al-code-sect-6-5-410/) (current text and codification history). **Status:** current code mirror; the 1965 text still needs archival verification.

**Story implication:** a 2025 estate cannot casually reopen a 1965 wrongful-death damages claim. The present conflict should therefore be about probate, record custody, reputation, consent, moral debt, or a new disclosure—not an invented live damages claim—unless a specialist identifies a surviving cause of action.

## 2. Present estate: plausible Alabama 2025 mechanisms

### Probate contest and deadline

**Documented law (Alabama, 2025 hypothesis):** For a will filed for probate on or after January 1, 2023, § 43-8-210(a)-(b) makes the newer contest division applicable and excludes §§ 43-8-190 and 43-8-199 for that class of proceeding. Section 43-8-215(a) provides the operative rule: a will may be contested **before probate or within 180 days after admission to probate** by an interested person, by written allegations of nonexecution, unsoundness of mind, or another valid objection. Sources: [§ 43-8-210](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-43/chapter-8/article-7/division-4/section-43-8-210/) and [§ 43-8-215](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-43/chapter-8/article-7/division-4/section-43-8-215/). These are current-code reproductions from a secondary publisher; the official codified portal was unavailable on 2026-10-30. Sections [§ 43-8-190](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-43/chapter-8/article-7/division-3/section-43-8-190/) and [§ 43-8-199](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-43/chapter-8/article-7/division-3/section-43-8-199/) should not be presented as the current general rule for a 2025-filed will.

**Story option:** Callie, as an heir or beneficiary whose economic position changes under the offered will, may be an interested person, but that conclusion depends on the will, intestacy comparison, and her actual property interest. A pre-probate filing and a post-admission contest are distinct routes; neither automatically starts discovery, compels disclosure, creates a custody hold, or fixes distribution. Her allegations must identify a recognized issue—execution defect, lack of testamentary capacity, undue influence, fraud, revocation, or similar—not merely that the will is insulting or morally manipulative.

**Open:** Which county probate court; whether and when the will is offered or admitted; Callie's exact intestate and testamentary interest; personal representative and letters date; applicable notice; venue/removal posture; and whether the selected county uses “caveat” as a local filing label.

### Valid versus defective alternate will

**Documented law (Alabama, current):** Under Ala. Code § 43-8-131, a will must be written, signed by the testator (or at direction/presence), and witnessed by at least two people. Section 43-8-132 provides self-proving formalities before an authorized officer. Sources: [§ 43-8-131](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-43/chapter-8/article-7/division-1/section-43-8-131/) and [§ 43-8-132](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-43/chapter-8/article-7/division-1/section-43-8-132/). **Status:** current statutory text reproduced by an unofficial legal publisher.

**Three usable options:**

- **Nonbinding expression:** Eli's letter/video states a wish or proposed moral allocation. It can motivate bargaining but cannot itself transfer estate property. Best fit with the adopted direction that living parties create the settlement.
- **Defective instrument:** a draft, unsigned writing, missing witness, or ambiguous paper is found in Leon's custody. It can create a contest issue and evidentiary fight, but it is not automatically a will. The defect must be specific and plausible.
- **Valid alternate will:** admissible only after proof of statutory execution, testamentary capacity, no undue influence/fraud, revocation analysis, custody/authentication, and procedural notice. It can be a real legal threat, but it should not appear as a last-minute moral solution.

The current treatment's “alternative will” is therefore a **proposed story mechanism**, not verified fact. A lawyer character should not say filing it “formalizes peace” without explaining probate consequences and the parties' ability to settle instead.

### Settlement, renunciation, and living-party control

**Documented law (Alabama, current):** Ala. Code § 43-8-250 governs contracts concerning succession and requires specified written evidence; § 43-8-251 addresses written renunciation of an interest. Sources: [§ 43-8-250](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/2020/title-43/chapter-8/article-9/section-43-8-250/) and [§ 43-8-251](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/2020/title-43/chapter-8/article-9/section-43-8-251/). **Status:** statute mirror; § 43-8-250 link is a 2020 text and must be checked for 2025 amendments.

**Story option:** After a contest is filed, interested adults may negotiate a written settlement: asset distribution, dismissal/withdrawal, releases, record custody, publication permissions, and privacy terms. That negotiation is not an automatic consequence of the contest window. Daniel and Sarah must consent for their own rights; Callie cannot give them a “veto” over all estate property unless the agreement gives it to them or another legal mechanism supplies it. If any beneficiary is a minor or incapacitated, court/protective procedures become important; the treatment presently says the twins are adults, but this should be confirmed. Distribution, final settlement, and any record/publication agreement must be timed and documented separately.

**Key distinction:** An estate settlement can bind signatories as a contract. It does not automatically erase public court filings, waive third-party rights, bind a non-signatory, or authorize publication of someone else's confidential communications.

## 3. Evidence custody, disclosure, recordings, and public access

### Custody chain

The treatment can plausibly make Leon a custodian of papers, a video, or a box, but custody is not the same as authority to disclose. For every item, specify: owner/client; original or copy; who possessed it; whether it was filed or sealed; chain of custody; authentication witness; privilege/work-product status; and whether a court order or settlement controls release.

### Attorney confidentiality after death

**Documented current rule:** The Alabama Judicial System's official [Rules of Professional Conduct](https://judicial.alabama.gov/library/RulesBarConduct) publish Rule 1.6's confidentiality framework. **Status:** current rule, not evidence of exact 1965 wording. The rule should not be used to assert that Leon may freely release Eli's old client materials, or that a personal representative automatically owns every privileged communication.

**Story option:** Leon can seek a probate/court order, obtain consent from authorized parties, release only nonprivileged historical material, or negotiate a redacted record. A dramatic “release everything” moment needs a legal and ethical decision point, not just a key.

### Conditional video / recording

**Documented current law:** Ala. Code § 13A-11-30 defines “eavesdrop” as recording or transmitting a private communication without consent of at least one participant, subject to statutory exceptions. Source: [§ 13A-11-30](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-13a/chapter-11/article-2/section-13a-11-30/) (current text). **Status:** current code mirror; recording made in another state, a two-party state, or by a nonparticipant can change the analysis.

**Story option:** Eli's staged video is easiest if he recorded himself, knowingly created it for post-death delivery, and Leon holds the file under written instructions. A secretly captured family conversation is a different and riskier device. Recording legality does not answer copyright, privacy, privilege, authentication, or publication questions.

### Public records and confidentiality

Official Alabama judiciary materials identify the [Alabama Rules of Court](https://judicial.alabama.gov/Library/RulesOfCourt) and the [probate-records access portal](https://alabamacourts.us/civil-court-records/probate). **Status:** official judiciary resources, but county implementation and online availability vary.

**Story option:** A filed will and probate docket may become accessible through the county court, while private settlement terms, attorney files, medical material, and sealed exhibits may remain restricted. “Public record” should not be used as a blanket answer for every box, recording, or historical file.

## Morgan Shaw — 2025 Alabama probate-clock diagnosis (checked 2026-10-30)

**Status and scope.** This is a dated research correction and plausibility test of the provisional season break. It is not legal advice, legal clearance, a county determination, or approved canon. The design hypothesis is a will filed for probate in Alabama in 2025; no county, will terms, admission status, party interests, or counsel is fixed. Current-law findings below must not be transposed to the 1965 line.

### Bottom line: no single Alabama “estate clock” supplies all the proposed gates

Alabama can supply a real contest clock, but not the break’s single cumulative clock as a legal object. For a will filed for probate on or after **January 1, 2023**, the newer contest division applies. Ala. Code § 43-8-210(a)-(b) (2025) says the division applies to those wills and that §§ 43-8-190 and 43-8-199 do not apply to them. The operative current contest provision is § 43-8-215: a will may be contested **before probate or within 180 days after admission to probate** by an interested person, on written allegations of nonexecution, unsoundness of mind, or another valid objection. The operative text begins: “A will, before the probate thereof or at any time within 180 days after the admission of such will to probate, may be contested by any interested person” by written allegations; it further says all interested persons must be parties, and the disability provision preserves a contest window in specified circumstances but “no more than 20 years” after probate. This is a litigation deadline, not a response deadline, custody deadline, disclosure deadline, distribution deadline, or publication-consent deadline.

Accordingly, the break may use an **invented dramatic clock** that places pressure around (1) pre-probate filing, (2) admission, (3) the 180-day contest period, (4) discovery/custody choices, and (5) later settlement. It must label those as story beats rather than imply that Alabama law makes them one sequence or that one filing compels a hold, production, publication, or settlement.

### Legally distinct gates

1. **Pre-probate caveat/contest.** Section 43-8-215(a) permits written contest allegations before probate in the court where the will is offered (or circuit court if properly removed). There is no single statutory “opening response” period in the text located here. A contestant needs a legally affected interest and a valid ground; moral objection alone is not enough. “Caveat” is a useful story label only if counsel confirms the selected county’s filing practice.

2. **Admission to probate.** Admission is a court event that changes the contest timing. It does not itself prove the will valid in every later dispute, authorize disclosure of private files, or order the family to negotiate. For a 2025-filed will, the relevant adult contest period is 180 days after admission under § 43-8-215(a), not the old six-month formulation in § 43-8-199.

3. **Notice.** Notice is a separate procedural question. The sources checked do not support a universal “notice starts the estate clock” proposition. Notice requirements vary by proceeding and county practice. Final-settlement notice is addressed separately in § 43-2-505; it is not the will-contest deadline. The Alabama Judicial System’s Rules of Court page identifies the official rules source, but the exact applicable probate rule and local procedure require a county-specific check.

4. **Interested-person standing.** Ala. Code § 43-8-1 defines “interested person” to include heirs, devisees, children, spouses, creditors, beneficiaries, and others with a property right or claim that may be affected, plus certain fiduciaries; the meaning depends on the proceeding. Callie’s standing cannot be assumed without knowing the will, intestacy comparison, any trust or creditor interest, and what relief she seeks.

5. **Post-admission contest.** For the 2025 hypothesis, § 43-8-215(a) supplies the 180-day period. Section 43-8-210 is a material currency correction: the prior memo’s reliance on §§ 43-8-190 and 43-8-199 as the current general rule was too broad. Those older provisions remain relevant to older transition cases, not as the clean rule for a will filed in 2025. A contest is not automatically a discovery or document-release order.

6. **Discovery.** A contested case may involve civil discovery, subpoenas, depositions, and court orders, but the research located no single Alabama statutory discovery period that can serve as the break’s fixed “response/hold” gate. Timing depends on the court, pleadings, scheduling orders, removal, and the actual claims. Do not have a filing automatically compel Leon to disclose an unfiled packet.

7. **Custody/hold.** “Hold,” preservation, privilege, client confidentiality, ownership, authentication, and a court-ordered injunction are different things. No generic Alabama probate statute located here creates a mandatory estate-wide custody hold for historical papers, recordings, or family photographs. A hold can be a negotiated term, ethical choice, preservation request, or case-specific order; the story must identify which.

8. **Distribution and final settlement.** Ala. Code § 43-2-501 (2025) permits final settlement after six months from the grant of letters, subject to statutory conditions. Section 43-2-505 supplies settlement notice (including publication and, for final settlement, at least 10 days’ notice to known adult distributees in Alabama and bond sureties). Section 43-2-506 permits settlement by consent without notice when its written-consent and debt/eligibility conditions are met. These are administration/settlement mechanisms, not the 180-day will-contest period and not publication permission for historical records.

9. **Settlement and court approval.** Probate settlement, a will-contest compromise, and a private family/record agreement are not interchangeable. Court involvement and approval depend on the instrument, parties, vulnerable beneficiaries, debts, and county procedure. A contract may bind its signatories; it cannot automatically bind a non-signatory or grant a signatory authority over another person’s name, image, voice, letters, or confidential communications.

10. **Publication/consent.** Publication or limited disclosure is independent of probate settlement. Neither a distribution, accounting, contest dismissal, nor payment term itself establishes consent to publish another person’s identifying material. Daniel and Sarah’s permissions must be mapped by person and item; the season break’s separation of money/accounting, asset authentication, image/name authority, and record permission is legally safer as a design constraint, but it is not a conclusion that either survivor has a universal veto over every estate asset.

### What the current evidence supports dramatically

**Option A — Contest-triggered, living-party settlement (strongest).** Callie files a pre-probate contest or contests within the 180-day post-admission period, if she has standing and a recognized ground. The filing creates litigation pressure, not automatic custody or disclosure. Discovery and a negotiated preservation/inspection arrangement then create the midseason pressure. The estate can later reach administration/final settlement under §§ 43-2-501, 43-2-505, and/or § 43-2-506, while a separate record/publication agreement is negotiated. This preserves the break’s living-party ending without claiming one legal clock.

**Option B — Valid instrument appears early (sharper but riskier).** A second will can matter only if execution, capacity, revocation, undue influence, custody, authentication, notice, and standing are litigated. It would create a contest issue, not a moral shortcut. Keep estate consequences and record consent separate. This option risks letting Eli’s instrument author the ending and should not be used as a last-minute solution.

**Non-legal dramatic-clock alternative.** Make the clock contractual and human: an executor, storage facility, insurer, archivist, publisher, or family mediator sets a documented inspection/return date, while counsel warns that missing it may affect bargaining or preservation—not statutory rights. The audience gets urgency without inventing an Alabama probate response or disclosure deadline.

### Explicit corrections and limits

The earlier memo’s statement that §§ 43-8-190 and 43-8-199 provide the current Alabama rule for the 2025 estate was too broad. For a will filed in 2025, § 43-8-210 directs attention to the post-2022 division and § 43-8-215’s 180-day rule. The earlier memo also treated “settlement” too generally: § 43-8-250 concerns contracts concerning succession, not a universal probate-settlement power, and it should not be cited as if it creates the season’s settlement clock. The earlier memo’s references to “notice,” “discovery,” “hold,” and “court approval” should be read as open procedural questions, not established linked gates.

Nothing checked establishes a fixed response period after a contest, a compulsory custody/disclosure stage, a universal publication-consent rule, or a required court approval for every private estate/record agreement. Nothing here establishes 1965 law. The 1965 line requires separate period-code, reported-decision, county-practice, and historical ethics research; current §§ 43-8-210 and 43-8-215 cannot be backdated.
