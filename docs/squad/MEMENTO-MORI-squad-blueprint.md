## Recommendation

**Team name: Canyon House Pictures.** This is the squad's fictional production-company identity. Its MEMENTO MORI development team is led by Paul Murphy as human creator and final creative authority. The agents use the fictional professional names below.

Model this as a small scripted television development company with a writers' room attached. Paul is the human creator and final creative authority. An AI showrunner integrates the work; a separate development producer challenges it; specialists develop the story and evidence supporting it.

Use six core specialists plus Squad's built-in Scribe. Add a legal research specialist and a pitch designer for defined assignments. These are functional development roles, not professional credentials or contractual screen credits.

## First assignment: development diagnosis

The first deliverable is a candid assessment with proposed solutions, not episode prose. It should identify strengths, contradictions, structural risks, missing research and the few decisions that block further development.

Specific questions raised by the supplied material:

1. **Chronology:** Choose exact historical and contemporary years, establish Eli and Leon's career stages and ages, the twins' birth year and present ages, Callie's age, and the duration of Eli's payments. Check the entire family chronology before casting assumptions or scripting.
2. **Two active timelines:** What does each protagonist want and do in each episode? Historical scenes need their own conflict and forward movement. They cannot exist solely to explain the contents of the next box Callie opens.
3. **Caleb's life:** Give Caleb choices, relationships, work and aspirations beyond the effect of his death on Eli. Develop his widow as a person with agency and an actual role in the decisions affecting her family. Identify what the twins know and want before Eli's estate summons them.
4. **Eli and Leon:** Test the source's suggestion of shared complicity against their different exposure to racist violence, institutional access and power. Their involvement need not be morally or materially symmetrical.
5. **The legal engine:** Establish what a will contest actually accomplishes in the selected jurisdiction and how it relates to a separately arranged release of information. Research the conditional video, trusts, charity, survivor arrangements and competing testamentary documents. Do not treat their validity as established by the treatment.
6. **Historical causation:** Specify the original legal dispute, Eli's maneuver, how information became exposed, who acted, what evidence survived, and what choices the survivors faced. Avoid vague legal cleverness standing in for a causal chain.
7. **The ending:** Assess whether the late alternative will gives Eli control again just after Callie rejects that control. Propose ways to seed it, alter its function or replace it, with the effect on theme made explicit. No revision is adopted automatically.
8. **Moral repetition:** The source links Callie's final compromise to Eli and Leon's earlier silence. Determine what is repeated, what changes, whose interests are protected, and what it costs the Caldwell family. A thematic parallel should survive scrutiny of the actual circumstances.
9. **Present-day stakes:** What can Callie lose or damage through each investigative choice? Establish an estate process and relationships capable of generating ten episodes rather than a sequence of explanations.
10. **Tone and motifs:** Preserve family wit and legal evasiveness without using racial terror as a punchline. Audit the award, photograph, pen, names and final phrase for setup, evolution and payoff. The source's Callie/Caleb visual echo should be tested for screen legibility. Log duplicated prose and prop-description inconsistencies as editorial issues.

Return a short decision memo with a recommendation and meaningful alternatives for each major fork. Paul should not need to arbitrate every line edit.

## Package definition

There is no universal mandatory packet or page count for every buyer. The following is the proposed definition of complete for this project. Lengths are working targets, adjustable to recipient requirements. Sundance's episodic development materials include a pilot, logline, synopsis, overview and creator statements; Screen Australia's guidance distinguishes a development/pitch bible from a more extensive production reference. [Sundance Episodic Program](https://www.sundance.org/programs/episodic-storytelling) · [Screen Australia story-document guidance](https://www.screenaustralia.gov.au/getmedia/133af42b-7abe-4ebc-a164-799c1c7a4614/Story-Documents-Drama.pdf)

### Presentation and reading materials

- **One-page series pitch:** Title, author, logline, premise, format, tone, central conflict and audience promise.
- **Development bible:** Approximately 15–25 pages covering the world, major characters and relationships, series engine, themes, timeline rules, season trajectory and ending. Include the Caldwell family's perspective and the moral consequences of the resolution.
- **Season treatment:** Approximately 8–12 pages telling the complete ten-episode story in compelling prose, including spoilers and the ending. Avoid unnecessary duplication with the bible.
- **Ten episode synopses:** Approximately 1–2 pages each, with a clear protagonist objective, conflict, historical and contemporary movement, turn and ending. Episode one must agree with the pilot.
- **Polished pilot screenplay:** One complete, consistently formatted Fountain (`.fountain`) source file and a reader PDF exported from that source, plus a short pilot synopsis. Approximate hour-long length is a working target, not a rigid conversion of pages to minutes.
- **Pitch deck:** Approximately 12–16 slides covering hook, world, characters, dual-timeline experience, season, tone, visual approach and creator perspective.
- **Oral pitch:** Short and longer versions, approximately 3–5 and 10–15 minutes, with likely development questions and grounded responses.
- **Creator statement and bio:** Draft from Paul's actual account of his connection to the material. Missing biography or motivation must remain clearly identified rather than invented.
- **Positioning and production overview:** Audience hypothesis, thematic comparisons, distinctive proposition, period and contemporary locations, double casting/age work, crowds, vehicles and other major cost drivers. No fabricated buyer interest, attachments, sales projections or precise budget.

### Internal development records

- Ten episode beat sheets and a pilot scene outline.
- Character, age and relationship ledger; story chronology and audience disclosure order.
- Clue/knowledge matrix, including evidence reliability and payoff.
- Historical dossier, legal research memo, source/claim register and intentional departures from fact.

The internal records support a concise presentation packet; they do not all need to be sent to every recipient. Final export should include editable sources and clean reader PDFs, with an editable deck and PDF export if requested. Actual export verification occurs when those artifacts are produced.

## Canon and evidence controls

Keep separate labels for:

- **Explicit brief:** Paul's stated requirements.
- **Source-backed:** Present in the original treatment but still open to deliberate development.
- **Proposed:** A creative option awaiting approval.
- **Approved canon:** A creative decision accepted by Paul.
- **Documented fact:** A factual claim supported by a traceable source.
- **Unverified / contested:** A claim or interpretation needing further work.

Fictional canon and historical evidence are different records. A fictional character's statement is not proof, and an approved dramatic invention is not a historical finding.

For each reveal record: clue ID; underlying event and date; discovery episode and scene; who knows before/after; audience belief before/after; reliability; plausible competing interpretation; character action caused; eventual payoff. Record whether a historical scene is objective dramatization, a character's recollection or a disputed reconstruction.

For each factual claim record: claim ID; jurisdiction and relevant date where applicable; source title, author, URL and page/section; what it actually supports; uncertainty; intended scene use. Every creative deliverable identifies the canon version it uses. Changes to dates, kinship, motives, legal mechanisms or the ending trigger an impact check across all affected documents.

## Definition of done

The package is ready for Paul's final review when all agreed documents are present; the pilot and episode summaries agree; ten episodes have distinct dramatic movement; every major reveal has setup and consequence; characters have credible agency; factual assertions are supported or marked as inventions/unknowns; the ending resolves the story's chosen questions; all editable screenplay deliverables are Fountain (`.fountain`); screenplay PDFs match their approved Fountain sources; no FDX files are included; and presentation files open and read correctly.

Unresolved professional legal clearance, genuine human historical/cultural consultation, biography questions and image rights are disclosed where applicable. AI review cannot be recorded as completed human consultation. No publishing, submissions, outreach or claimed attachments are part of this team-definition assignment.
